## Recurrence results and interpretation

The fixed partition is8bars of1152ticks. Each note belongs to its onset bar;
its sounding gate is retained even across a bar boundary. Original channel
note-on order is preserved, not sorted by note-off. This is not voice extraction
or a learned phrase segmentation. Empty-bar pairs are excluded and single-onset
bars are counted explicitly: one-note relative pitch trivially equals zero.

Equal-output means,10 outputs per condition (timing context off → on):

- Adjacent same-pitch fraction:0.190241 →0.226704.
- Nonadjacent bar relative-pitch edit similarity:0.301384 →0.273336.
- Bar onset-count coefficient of variation:0.400761 →0.379200.
- Nonadjacent exact rhythm matches and exact pitch-plus-rhythm matches:zero in
  both conditions. Exact-tick equality is strict; zero does not prove no audible
  recurrence or coherent structure. No quantization/tolerance was introduced.

Thus more immediate same-pitch repetition can coexist with *less* measured
nonadjacent pitch-pattern similarity. A lower onset-count variation describes
more uniform density, not better musical pacing. This analysis supplies separate
descriptors, not a preferred direction or a good-music score. A synthetic test
also holds same-pitch rate constant while changing cross-bar recurrence.

Pooled descriptive correlations among these20 already observed mixed-condition
outputs are -0.149630 (same-pitch versus nonadjacent pitch similarity) and
0.191675 (same-pitch versus onset-count variation). No significance, independence,
causality or population claim. Repeated runs reproduce all non-timestamp results.
A prospective next question is how tolerant rhythmic motifs and phrase-level
recurrence relate to human judgments, with sensitivity analysis on synthetic
examples before new candidate selection. Do not optimize the current descriptors
and then call those same outputs a fresh quality test.
