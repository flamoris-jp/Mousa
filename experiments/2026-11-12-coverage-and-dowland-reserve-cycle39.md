# Cycle39: more rhythm training and a rights-reviewed melodic reserve

## Actual increase, fixed comparison

Groove rhythm training increased from 35 recordings / 19,409 events to 58 unique recordings / 32,141 events. The same six training drummers, unchanged six probability formulas and sixteen previously observed evaluation recordings were retained. The 23 added recordings were chosen by source hash and ID before fitting, not by prediction scores. Forty evaluation-session recordings and the known byte-identical alias remain excluded.

The new bounded 60-work / 60,000-event profile leaves all historical models unchanged. Full-source integrity and 40,302 cross-split checks passed the fixed admission screen; weak musical patterns remain documented, not certified independent. Old predictions reproduced exactly in 96 work-condition comparisons. Initial and repeated runs matched except CPU time. Independent reviews reconstructed selection, checked 576 exact synthetic probability comparisons and manually reset context counts, and verified 276 result identities.

All six models improved event-pooled test prediction after expansion. Two quotient/residual variants slightly worsened event-pooled validation prediction, including drummer8, while recording-macro validation averages improved. These are reused observed cohorts, not new independent evidence. Training event share became more concentrated in drummer5 (37.78% to 44.81%); provided style labels grew from 12 to 19 but are not verified independent genres. Recording count, event count and musical content changed together. No musical-preference benefit is claimed.

See `2026-11-12-groove-cycle39-coverage-overview.md`, the frozen plan, actual-use ledger and independent reviews for all conditions, denominators, limits and the recorded pre-fit prototype failure.

## New melodic material is reserved, not trained

Two Dowland pieces, *Unquiet Thoughts* and *Come Again*, were examined against the 1597 first-book source and the contributor's explicit public-domain dedication. They belong to one shared book/contributor family, not two independent cohorts. Original MIDI files retain all voices, timing and rests. The Cantus track is the selected technical analysis view, not a replacement of the full original. Their fixed comparison against current training, observed diagnostics, known blocked references and each other produced no admission flag. They remain untrained and predictively unscored; any later use requires a new frozen assignment and comparison plan. The detailed rights ledger records the separate composition, edition and typesetting/MIDI provenance and remaining limits.
