# Interpreting source similarity screens

## Three different questions

1. **Evaluation independence:** can a fixed experiment legitimately use a source
   without a prohibited relationship to training or already inspected material?
2. **Generated-output reproduction risk:** does an output need closer review
   because it resembles a source?
3. **Musical recurrence:** does a generated line repeat or vary its own material?

One numeric threshold cannot answer all three. None is a copyright verdict or a
measurement of musical attractiveness. A failed research eligibility gate may
be conservative without making the underlying historical source unlawful.

## Current conservative gate stays fixed

The existing first-predictive-evaluation plans stop on any shared eight-onset
pitch-interval window, or whole-line interval edit similarity at least0.80.
Telemann1692 remains blocked under its saved plan. Its rights snapshot is not
revoked, its matching spans are not deleted, and other movements or transpositions
must not be treated as unrelated fresh sources. Rhythm differences do not waive
that plan's pitch-only rule. Report unscreenable references as missing coverage,
not as similarity zero.

## What synthetic sensitivity experiments can establish

A constructed transformation has known mechanical provenance. It can show that
an implementation is invariant to transposition, changes under rhythmic edits,
or misses a match after an inserted note. It can expose collisions between short
ordinary patterns and the strict gate. It cannot establish how often those
collisions occur in music, whether listeners perceive a meaningful resemblance,
or whether any passage is legally protectable.

Use fixtures whose relations and transformations are specified before computing
scores. Include both ordinary scales/repeated notes and distinctive constructed
interval/rhythm sequences, but treat those names as fixture descriptions, not
expert labels or a gold-standard judgment of originality. Record all outcomes,
not just examples supporting a proposed metric. Keep distinct tests for global
transposition, tempo scaling, local timing perturbation, insertion/deletion,
short sequences, empty inputs and mixed-length comparisons.

Report pitch-only, timing-only and combined descriptors side by side. Exact
rational timing, normalized timing and tolerance-based timing answer different
questions. If tempo normalization is explored, disclose loss of absolute speed.
If edit distance is used, disclose length effects and how empty/short inputs are
handled. Do not label a failed synthetic metric result as a failure to detect
plagiarism, or a zero match as originality clearance.

## Requirements before any future changed eligibility policy

- State the proposed use: source independence versus output review versus
  within-output recurrence. Do not silently transfer a rule between purposes.
- Freeze the metric, normalization, thresholds, minimum evidence and handling of
  missing/unsupported references before examining the proposed new evaluation
  source. Keep composition, arrangement and contributor families together.
- Validate implementation against synthetic transformations separately from
  real-music calibration. Real-music labels require a justified review process;
  anonymous algorithm scores are not human judgments.
- Reserve different material for calibration and final evaluation. Material
  already used to diagnose the old rule is observed, even if no model NLL was
  computed. A revised protocol cannot make Telemann1692 untouched again.
- Preserve previous results and blocked decisions. Disclose policy changes and
  examine sensitivity rather than choosing a threshold to pass a desired work.
- Keep rights/provenance review and human listening separate. Better matching
  does not authorize training, release or use of uncertain-license data.

No threshold or source admission changes are enacted by this document.
